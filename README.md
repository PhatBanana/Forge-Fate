# Forge & Fate

**[Open the app →](https://phatbanana.github.io/Forge-Fate/)**

A D&D 5e character builder that tells you *why*. It rates every species against
every class, recommends your next feat or ability score improvement with the
reasoning spelled out, plans every ability score improvement to level 20, prints
a real character sheet, and imports characters from D&D Beyond.

Nothing to install and no account. Your characters stay in your browser, and
sharing one puts it in the link rather than on anyone's server.

**It works offline.** Visit once and it keeps working with no signal — which is
where a lot of tables are. On a phone you can add it to your home screen.

## What it does

It asks two things on your first visit: which Player's Handbook your table uses,
and whether to start from a blank sheet or a worked example. The example is a
level 5 Battle Master with every panel full, so you can see what the rankings do
before choosing anything.

Four tabs: **Builder**, **Character sheet**, **Species × Class**, **Characters**.
The Builder is one page — Identity, Abilities, Equipment, Skills & options,
Feats — with a rail of anchors down the top carrying a count of the choices each
is still waiting on, so "what have I not finished?" is answered without
scrolling. The catalogues fold: what you have taken is always visible, and
exactly one list of what you have not is open at a time.

**Every choice is ranked where you make it**, with the reasons that moved the
number: skills, class options, spells, feats and ability score improvements, all
scored against your actual character and applied with one click.

**Pinned beside the character**, on every part of it: what your character is
right now, what is still unchosen and where, a level-by-level plan to 20 with
each step re-checked against the one before it, and damage per round charted
against **level** — is this build front-loaded or does it come good at eleven —
with the against-armor-class curve a click away.

**Species × Class.** The matrix — 43 lineages × 13 classes, every pairing rated
Excellent / Solid / Situational / Avoid with the reasoning attached and
clickable to load that pairing. Its own tab rather than part of the Builder,
because loading a pairing resets scores, defenses, feats and weapons — not
something to put inside the page where you are editing them.

**Proficiencies.** All 18 skills with their modifiers, what granted each one,
and both passive scores. It knows expertise, Jack of All Trades, Remarkable
Athlete, Reliable Talent and Observant.

**Known, prepared, and the difference.** A Wizard keeps their spellbook and ticks
what is prepared today, counted against Intelligence + level; the printed page
marks the live ones. Every other preparer draws from their whole class list, so
what they record already *is* the prepared list. Cantrips are never prepared.

**Class features and options.** Every class's features by level, and every
subclass's — 175 subclasses across 864 entries — with the next level shown so you
can see what is coming. The choices a feature hands you (Fighting Styles, Pact
Boons, Invocations, Metamagic, maneuvers) are ranked against your build: best
three shown, the rest one click away, and what you have taken never truncated.

**Starting equipment.** Each class's kit as the book gives it, in both editions —
*"(a) chain mail or (b) leather armor, longbow, and 20 arrows"*. Where the book
says *any martial melee weapon* you get a list of exactly those. Taking it
dresses your character: armor on, best weapon in hand, pack filled, and 2024's
starting gold in your purse.

**Going up a level.** Type the new level and the app tells you what it gave you —
the hit points, the features by name — and what it is now waiting on, with a
button to the section that makes each choice. You can roll this level's hit die
right there, or take the average.

**Inventory and encumbrance.** The whole Player's Handbook equipment list,
searchable, with cost and weight. Weight counts everything a hand-written sheet
forgets: gear, the weapon in your hand, the armor on your body, and the coins in
your purse. Carrying capacity is Strength × 15.

**Equipment and attacks.** Equip weapons and the app works out how you fight —
you never tell it "I am a two-handed build", it reads that from the greatsword —
then shows the attack line, itemised. 2024 weapon mastery is offered ranked.

**Spells.** 344 spells with slots by level, how many you know or prepare, and the
picks you control ranked. Warlock pact slots are shown as the separate resource
they are, and the spells a subclass hands you arrive always prepared and free of
every count. A multiclass caster gets one save DC per casting class, and each
spell says which of them it is cast at.

**Damage per round.** Sustained and nova output, itemised, with a curve across
every target AC you might face — the only honest way to answer whether Great
Weapon Master's −5/+10 pays, since it is worth it below a break-even AC and a
trap above it. Casters get a real number, not a zero.

**Build review.** Mistakes, not unfinished business: an odd primary score,
Constitution too low to hold concentration, a combat feat that does nothing with
your weapons, a −5/+10 feat with no way to get advantage, prerequisites you no
longer meet, armor you are not proficient with, Dexterity wasted under an armor
cap, a skill pick your background already gave you, a Warlock who blasts without
Agonizing Blast, a spell list made mostly of things you cannot concentrate on at
once.

Choices you simply have not made yet are *not* listed here — the section counts
already show those, and a review that opens with nine complaints about an
untouched character teaches you to stop reading it.

**Characters.** A roster: switch between them, duplicate before trying an idea,
rename, delete, with AC, hit points and damage for each at a glance. There is no
save button — everything is saved as you type — and undo, per character, is the
way back. A character that somehow will not open never takes the app down with
it: you are offered the whole roster as a file before anything is discarded.

**Share links.** A character packs into the link itself, so there is no upload
and nothing lands in anyone's logs. Opening someone's link offers you their
character rather than replacing yours, and carries the ruleset with it.

**The character sheet, and it is the printed page.** One layout, not two: what
you read on screen is what comes out of the printer, box for box. The **build
summary** is a separate thing worth printing — the damage curve, the plan to 20,
the review — useful while planning and noise at the table.

**Dice.** Every modifier on the sheet is a button. Click a skill, a save, an
ability, initiative or an attack and it rolls, showing the working:
`d20: 14 +5 = 19`. Advantage is a switch, and both dice are shown so you can see
the one you dropped. Damage rolls from the attack line, with a **crit** button
that doubles the dice and not the bonus. A hit die spends and heals in one press.
A death save applies the two things everybody misplays: a natural 20 puts you
back on your feet, and a natural 1 is two failures. The last twenty rolls are
kept. None of the buttons print — the numbers do, because they are the sheet.

**In play.** Session tracking lives in the sheet's own boxes rather than a panel
beside it: hit points, hit dice, spell and pact slots, death saves, and every
class resource from Rage and Ki to Arcane Recovery. A quiver sits under the
attacks it feeds, counted in arrows, with the rule for searching the battlefield
afterwards as a button. Rests do the real thing — a short rest returns Pact
Magic, and hit dice are spent *during* one rather than handed back by it. It all
survives a refresh, and undo will not rewind your hit points.

**Your turn, tracked.** Action, bonus action, reaction and how far you have
walked, on the sheet beside your armor class. The button says **New turn**
rather than "end turn" on purpose: all four come back when your turn *starts*,
the reaction included, and a tracker that refreshed them when you finished would
hand back a reaction you spent on somebody else's turn. Movement counts against
the speed the sheet prints — armor penalty and magic boots included — and Dash
adds your speed again rather than doubling what is left, so twenty feet in you
have forty left, not twenty. Dash costs you nothing here, because it is an
action for most, a bonus action with Cunning Action, and free for a Tabaxi.

**Potions and scrolls get used up.** Say how many you have; drinking one spends
it and, for the four healing potions, rolls the dice and applies the hit points
in one press. A Spell Scroll has a box for which spell is on it, because the
books have no "Scroll of Invisibility" — they have a Spell Scroll (2nd Level),
and the spell is written on the scroll. Anything else is spent and logged, and
what it does is left to its description: the app has no model for a Potion of
Speed's haste and will not pretend to one.

**Counters for what the books here do not have.** Every class resource is
already tracked — Rage, Ki, Sorcery Points, Channel Divinity, Lay on Hands, all
of it, at the right maximum for your level and back on the right rest. For
anything from a supplement this app cannot reproduce, name your own: Theros
piety, Ravnica renown, a pool your DM wrote. A **pool** starts full and is spent
down; a **score** starts at nothing and is built up, and can be set to survive
every rest, which is what piety needs.

**Conditions and exhaustion.** All fourteen conditions as toggles with what each
one does written beside the switch — everybody forgets that being prone gives
their ranged attacks disadvantage. Exhaustion is a six-level track, so clicking
the fourth circle means you are on four and every effect at and below it is
listed. On paper, only what is actually on you prints.

**Running a game.** The app has two modes: **Create** — the Builder, the sheet,
Species × Class, your characters and bestiary — and **Play**, the battle screen,
which owns the whole window. Put your party in from the roster and whatever
they have run into from **334 SRD monster stat blocks**, roll initiative, and
work down the order. Click any monster for its full stat block, laid out the
way the books lay one out, and it prints.

The battle screen borrows its shape from squad-tactics games, because fifth
edition shares the bones of one — a grid, a turn order, an action economy,
classes. A strip of unit tiles runs across the top of the map with initiative,
name and a hit bar; an action bar sits along the bottom with the active
character's action, bonus action and reaction as spendable pips, their
movement draining as their token is dragged, and End turn.

The part worth knowing: **a character in the fight is not a copy of them.** Their
hit points in the tracker are the hit points on their sheet — the same number,
not two that have to be kept in step — so the damage you deal on the DM's screen
is there when the player looks at their own. Monsters keep their own, rolled from
the stat block's dice or taken as the printed average, and you can type over
either. Three goblins become Goblin A, B and C so it is clear which one died.

Ending a turn is the other half. When the order comes round to a character, the
app gives them back their action, bonus action, reaction and movement — the rule
their sheet already tracks, applied at the moment the rules apply it rather than
when somebody remembers.

**What this fight will do.** Once both sides are in, the app tells you how the
fight is likely to go — how much your party puts out against *these* monsters'
armor, how much comes back against *this* party's, and who runs out of hit
points first. It is built on the same damage model the Builder uses, so it knows
your real weapons and feats rather than a level.

It deliberately does **not** grade the fight "deadly" or "medium". Those words
come from an XP-threshold table that is Dungeon Master's Guide content, not SRD,
and this app does not reproduce what it has no licence to. Each monster's own XP
*is* in the SRD, so that total is shown beside the model. What the model leaves
out is named on the page — a dragon's breath weapon is on a recharge and is not
counted, and it says so rather than quietly rounding it in or out.

**Pop it out.** Any row in the order opens in its own window — a character's
full sheet, live and editable, or a monster's stat block — so you can park it on
a second screen and leave the tracker where it is. It is not a copy: it is the
same sheet, so a hit point changed in the window changes in the tracker as you
type. Where a browser blocks the window, or on a phone where there is nowhere to
put one, you get a draggable panel instead and nothing else changes.

The monsters are **SRD 5.1**. There is no licensed machine-readable source for
the 2024 bestiary yet — the API that serves the rest of this app carries three
creatures — so rather than invent one, a 2024 table gets the 5.1 stat blocks and
is told why, on the page, where it matters.

**A dungeon, from a seed.** Rooms, corridors and doors, generated and drawn on
a 5-foot grid, and it prints. The seed is the map: type the same one and you get
the same dungeon, so `the sunken abbey` in your notes is a map you can get back
next week rather than a picture you have to keep. Rooms are numbered left to
right so "the third room" means something to everyone at the table. Three map
sizes, and **zero rooms is a blank grid** — paint floor and walls to build your
own.

**Terrain, and height.** Paint pillars, rocks, trees, water and rubble onto
squares; raise a ledge or sink a pit in steps. It all lives with the fight, so
the battlefield you set survives a refresh. Walls, pillars, rocks and trees
block sight; water and rubble are difficult ground — shown, not policed.

**Line of sight.** A toggle draws sight lines from whoever is selected — solid
when clear, dashed where cut — and says the same in words: who they can see,
who they cannot, and who has half cover (+2 AC) behind something. Height
counts: a ridge hides two people on the flat, an archer a step up shoots over
the mid-field rock, and the same rock still hides whoever crouches behind it.

**Areas of effect.** Place a sphere, cube, cone or line on the map — a wall of
fire, a cloudkill — with a label and a duration in rounds. It stays where it
was cast while everyone moves, burns a round down each time the top of the
order comes back, and lists who is standing in it. What it does to them stays
the table's business.

**Simulate the fight.** Beyond the expected-value forecast, run the encounter
a thousand times: monster dice rolled for real through the app's dice engine,
your side dealing its modelled damage per round. Out the other side — how often
the party wins, how long it typically takes, and each character's chance of
hitting the floor. The variance is the point: a fight that is fine on averages
can still drop the wizard one time in four, and that is the number a DM
balancing an encounter actually needs.

**Three panels, like a virtual tabletop.** The Table is laid out the way a tool
you run a session from should be: turn order pinned down the left, the map in
the middle, and whoever you are looking at down the right. Drag the dividers to
resize, collapse either rail to a strip, and it remembers how you left it. Click
anybody in the turn order to bring them up on the right — a character as a
compact card of the numbers that matter mid-fight, a monster as its full stat
block. Anything can still be popped out into its own window.

**Tokens on it.** Put the fight on the map and drag people around. Each square
is five feet and a diagonal counts as five like every other step, so dragging a
character's token **spends their movement** — the same movement their sheet
tracks, so the map and the turn tracker are two views of one number rather than
two that have to be kept in step. The party is filled, monsters are outlined,
whose turn it is has a ring, and anyone at nought is dashed. It all prints.

**Monsters you made.** Under **Characters → Bestiary**: find the stat block that
is nearly right, copy it, and change whatever is wrong with it — the name, hit
points, armor class, speeds, ability scores, challenge rating, and the traits and
actions in full. Experience and proficiency bonus follow the rating, so they
cannot drift apart from it, and the stat block is drawn beside the fields as you
type. There is no blank creator on purpose: nobody builds a monster from nothing,
and every field you do not touch is one the SRD already got right.

Copying leaves the original alone, and your monsters are their own store — a
separate file, a separate export — so clearing out old characters never touches
them, and a bestiary can be handed to whoever is running next week without the
party going with it. The Table's search covers both, yours first and tagged, so a
monster you saved last week is one keystroke away mid-fight.

**Compare.** Two characters side by side, both damage figures read at the same
target AC and both curves overlaid so you can see *where* one overtakes the
other, then the feats, skills, options and spells that differ.

**A portrait**, in the box the sheet has for one, and it prints. Large photos are
resized automatically so a picture cannot fill up your browser's storage and stop
your characters saving. Share links leave it out, or they would be too long to
paste.

**Magic items.** The whole list — 371 entries across weapons, armor and shields,
wondrous items, rings, staffs, wands, rods, potions, scrolls and artifacts —
grouped the way the books group them. Where an item changes a number, it changes
the number, with a line on your sheet saying where the bonus came from.
Attunement slots are enforced and an unattuned item does nothing, with the panel
saying so rather than leaving you to wonder why your AC did not move. Anything
outside the list can be written in by name.

**Rules text.** Every spell and magic item leads with a one-line verdict — the
thing that helps you choose — and a **Full description** underneath with the
SRD's own words for the 319 spells and 361 items it covers.

## Which ruleset

**Both**, and you can switch at any time. The two are genuinely different shapes,
not a reskin:

|  | 2014 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Ability increases | Species | **Background** |
| Species give | Increases + traits | Traits only |
| Feats | One flat list | Origin / General / Fighting Style / Epic Boon, with level prerequisites |
| Free feat | Variant Human, Custom Lineage | Every background, plus the Human's Versatile |
| Subclass at | Level 1, 2 or 3, by class | **Level 3, every class** |
| Level 19 improvement | Any feat or ASI | **An Epic Boon** |
| Classes | Thirteen, including the Artificer | Twelve — the Artificer is not in that book |
| Subclasses | Every published one, 117 in all | The four per class that book carries |

Under 2014 there is also a **Tasha's custom origin** toggle, which lets ability
increases float wherever you want them. Under 2024 that is simply the rule, so
the toggle is hidden.

Switching clears anything that does not exist in the other book and **tells you
what it changed** rather than quietly rewriting your character.

Content spans the Player's Handbook plus the commonly-allowed expansions
(Xanathar's, Tasha's, Volo's, Mordenkainen's, Eberron, Theros, Ravenloft,
Fizban's, Wild Beyond the Witchlight, Strixhaven, Wildemount). Sources are
labelled throughout, so you can tell at a glance whether a pick needs a
conversation with your DM.

## Importing from D&D Beyond

Paste a character's URL or its JSON and it comes across: species, background,
classes, subclasses, ability scores, feats, spells and the whole pack — the
weapons in hand, the armor worn, the items attuned, the gear and the purse.

The character has to be set to **public** in its D&D Beyond privacy settings.
Pasted JSON is offered because D&D Beyond's server will not talk to a web page
directly; the app shows you the exact link to open and you paste what comes back.

Ability scores arrive as base scores plus the choices stacked on them rather than
one total, which is what lets the optimizer tell you which past choice to make
differently. Anything unrecognised — a homebrew feat, an unsupported class — is
reported rather than silently dropped. You can export any character to a file too.

## What it does not do

**The numbers that are models, and say so.**

- **The scores are opinions with reasoning attached, not a simulation.** They
  encode one defensible reading of 5e optimization. The reasoning is shown so
  you can disagree with a specific rule rather than an opaque number.
- **The plan to 20 is taken one step at a time**, which is how ASIs are actually
  taken, but it does not search for the best possible ordering.
- **Damage is one round, one target, in range.** No movement, no positioning, no
  reaction damage, no resistances. Advantage is a switch you set, area spells use
  a target count you set, and monster saves are estimated from tier the same
  rough way target AC is.
- **AC and hit points show their working**, but cannot know the situational half:
  Shield, Mage Armor, cover.

**What the licence covers.** The SRD is what can be checked; everything past it
is written from the books, and the app says which is which.

- **All 120 published subclasses have their features, but only twelve are
  verified.** The twelve the SRD covers are checked against it in both editions;
  the other 108 come from books no licensed source carries. The 55 beyond those
  are this project's own — 19 for the published classes and 36 for its own four
  — off by default, and never presented as published.
- **Four classes of the app's own**, behind the same switch: the Reckoner,
  the Harrier, the Marshal and the Adept. Each is measured against the
  published classes' damage band by a test that recomputes the band rather
  than pinning it, so none of them can quietly outdamage the books.
- **Subclass spell lists are granted for 2014 only.** A Life Cleric, Devotion
  Paladin and Fiend Warlock get theirs. 2024 revised every one of those lists
  and no licensed source carries the revisions, so a 2024 character is granted
  nothing rather than handed the old table under a new label.
- **The spell list is the SRD plus a few from later books.** 344 spells, every
  full caster covered at every level they reach. Toll the Dead, Absorb Elements
  and the smites are in because they change how those classes play. About one
  spell in nine carries no verdict and is marked *unrated* rather than given a
  number that means nothing.
- **Artificer infusions are not modelled**, and three class resources are
  2014-only: Wild Shape, Divine Sense and Flash of Genius, each for a reason the
  app states.

**Recorded rather than computed**, which is a smaller gap than being missing.

- **All 371 magic items are listed; 96 of them change a number.** The rest are
  recorded, attuned and printed with a description. Where an item needs a model
  the app does not have — resistance to a damage type, a spell cast from
  charges, a critical hit that becomes a normal one — it says what the item does
  and keeps the guess out of your numbers.
- **A Cloak and a Ring of Protection are added together.** Whether your table
  allows that is a question for your DM, and the item says so.
- **Tools and languages are tracked but not ranked**, because outside a few cases
  like thieves' tools there is no honest way to rank a mason's tools against a
  cook's utensils.
- **Only base-class resources are counted.** Superiority dice and domain features
  are printed rather than tracked. Anything from a supplement — piety, renown —
  can be added as a counter of your own, which is the honest answer where
  reproducing the table is not something this project can do.
- **The turn tracker counts, it does not rule.** It knows you have an action and
  will not tell you whether what you did needed one; it is a tally you keep, not
  a referee.
- **Thrown ranges are shown, not enforced.**

**And three limits of scope.**

- **Prepared spells are today's list, not a diary.** Change them and yesterday's
  choice is gone; this is a builder, not a logbook.
- **Multiclassing works but is not advised.** Levels, subclasses and the narrower
  proficiencies a dip grants all combine correctly, but the app will not propose
  a dip for you.
- **Epic Boons stop at 20 like everything else**, so a Boon that raises a score
  past the usual ceiling is scored on its effect instead.
